# Northeast India and Disaster Early Warning: Problems Where Indian-Language Voice AI Could Help

Research date: 2026-10-30. About 15 search/fetch calls. Figures are labelled by date. Disaster tolls change daily, so treat any single ASDMA number as a snapshot. Context: the participant is at IIIT Manipur and has done earlier disaster-risk monitoring work.

## Q1. Disaster early warning: last-mile gaps (Assam floods, NE landslides, Sikkim GLOF, CAP/SACHET, cell broadcast)

### Takeaway
India has a working forecast-to-alert pipeline. NESAC FLEWS covers Assam floods, and SACHET (based on CAP) has sent more than 134 billion SMS in 19+ languages. Cell broadcast was tested nationwide in May 2026. The documented failures in the Northeast are mostly about sensing, institutions and connectivity: no early-warning system at South Lhonak, a late dam-gate order, and villages with no mobile coverage. Language comes second. Two openings remain for voice: alerts that are read aloud in local languages that the government voice mandate has not yet reached, and two-way voice channels for reporting and help requests, which today's broadcast-only systems lack.

### Cited Findings
**Sikkim GLOF, 3–4 Oct 2023**
- Down To Earth (4 Oct 2023) reported that no early-warning system had been installed at South Lhonak Lake, although the lake had been known to be vulnerable for more than a decade. A joint NDMA/SSDMA team had assessed it in the second week of September 2023. The article describes only a general alert and evacuation of low-lying areas after water levels rose. It does not mention sirens, SMS or phone calls. — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/natural-disasters/sikkim-floods-why-wasn-t-early-warning-system-set-up-at-glacial-lake-known-to-be-highly-vulnerable-experts-ask-92116)
- Seismic data put the moraine failure at 22:13 on 3 Oct, and the flood reached Chungthang at about 00:30 on 4 Oct. That is roughly 2 hours of potential lead time. — [Eos Landslide Blog](https://eos.org/thelandslideblog/sikkim-glof-october-2023)
- Critics say the order to open the Dikchu dam gates came at about 2:00 AM on 4 Oct, which was too late. Teesta-III operators said the flood overtook them before they could open the gates, and the Teesta-III dam was destroyed. — [EastMojo report/recommendations, 23 Nov 2023](https://eastmojo.com/sikkim/2023/11/23/report-and-recommendations-on-the-glof-disaster-of-teesta-valley/); [Eos](https://eos.org/thelandslideblog/sikkim-glof-october-2023)
- Sikkim government figures: 46 dead, more than 77 missing, 88,400 affected. Penn State gave 42 deaths as of 8 Nov 2023, so the totals vary by date. — [search summary of The Wire / PSU sources](https://thewire.in/environment/sikkim-glof-triggered-flood-in-teesta-because-warnings-remained-neglected); [PSU IEE blog](https://iee.psu.edu/news/blog/glacier-lake-outburst-floods-loss-life-and-infrastructure)
- One analysis says an EWS near the lake was temporarily inactive because of extreme weather. This conflicts with DTE's report that no EWS had been installed. — [Rising Asia Journal](https://www.rajraf.org/article/teesta-disaster-a-human-made-catastrophe-and-the-lessons-learnt/1125); contradicted by [DTE](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/natural-disasters/sikkim-floods-why-wasn-t-early-warning-system-set-up-at-glacial-lake-known-to-be-highly-vulnerable-experts-ask-92116)

**Assam floods**
- 2022: about 54.6 lakh people were affected across 32 districts at the peak, per the ASDMA bulletin cited by Deccan Herald. About 90% of Silchar town was under water, and roughly 3 lakh people there faced shortages of food, water and medicine. The death toll was 127 per Scroll, while Wikipedia gives 200+ over a longer window. — [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/world/south-asia/273050/assam-floods-toll-tops-100-as-5.5-million-affected); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1026826/assam-floods-toll-rises-to-108-situation-remains-critical-in-silchar-town); [Wikipedia: 2022 Silchar Floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Silchar_Floods)
- 2024: early-July snapshots put about 11–16 lakh people affected across about 23 districts. The seasonal toll from floods, storms, lightning and landslides reached 116 in one count. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fassam%2Fassam-flood-situation-worsens-eight-dead-over-16-lakh-hit-3091792); [News Arena](https://www.newsarenaindia.com/states/floods-in-assam-kill-38-displace-over-11-lakh/15782)
- FLEWS (NESAC + ASDMA) started as a pilot in Lakhimpur in 2009 and now covers all flood-prone districts, issuing alerts at revenue-circle level. Reported lead times vary by source: NESAC says 12–36 h, a 2024 report says 24–36 h, and an older ASDMA page says 7–18 h. Reported success rates are also inconsistent: an average of 75%, and 86% for 2017 when partial hits are included. — [NESAC DMS](https://nesac.gov.in/scientific-programmes/disaster-management-support/); [ASDMA FLEWS](https://asdma.gov.in/project_flood_warning.html); [ETV Bharat 2024](https://etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/centre-utilises-space-applications-to-identify-wetlands-in-assam-enn24080806648)

**Landslides: Mizoram, Cyclone Remal, May 2024**
- 29 people died in Aizawl district landslides, including 14 at a stone quarry. Eight members of one family were swept away at Chawnpui. Aizawl was cut off for hours after a slide on NH-6. The state had closed offices in response to the IMD cyclone warning, so a warning did exist, but it was not tied to specific slopes or locations. — [News On AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/death-toll-in-multiple-landslides-in-mizorams-aizawl-district-climbed-to-29-after-two-more-bodies-recovered); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fmizoram%2Fcyclone-remal-impact-31-deaths-in-mizoram-assam-9-others-missing-3041130)

**National alert infrastructure**
- SACHET is a C-DOT system based on CAP and is operational in all states and UTs. It has sent more than 134 billion SMS alerts in 19+ Indian languages. This figure is from a PIB-sourced report and covers SMS only, not cell broadcast. — [TaxGuru (PIB)](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/india-deploys-mobile-alert-system-due-rising-disaster-preparedness.html); [IANS, May 2026](https://ianslive.in/govt-launches-cell-broadcast-alert-system-for-real-time-disaster-warnings--20260502131531)
- The first nationwide cell broadcast test ran in May 2026 in English, Hindi and regional languages. It reached only phones with test channels enabled. I found no published full list of supported cell broadcast languages. — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/amp/en/bharat/ndma-tests-cell-broadcast-message-to-alert-citizens-on-severe-disaster-enn26050201915); [Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/govt-tests-nationwide-mobile-emergency-alert-system-to-safeguard-citizens)
- DoT mandate, reported 28 Sep 2024: feature phones must support auto-readout (text-to-speech) of emergency messages in Hindi and English, adding 4 more Indian languages each year. Manufacturers say feature phones lack the memory for text-to-audio conversion. The Indian Wireless Telegraphy (Cell Broadcasting Service for Disaster Alerts) Act, 2023 requires cell broadcast capability on all phones sold. — [Drishti IAS summary](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/emergency-alert-messages-in-cell-phones/print_manually)
- Anecdote from a forum, not data: one recipient of the May 2026 test said a message in an unfamiliar South Indian language was confusing. — [Team-BHP](https://www.team-bhp.com/forum/shifting-gears/307941-ndma-launch-cell-broadcast-alert.html)
- Comparator from Sri Lanka, not India: during Cyclone Ditwah, critical alerts went out mostly in English and Sinhala, with Tamil content delayed or omitted. — [arXiv 2601.18308](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18308)
- In 2022, Google's flood alerts in India were reportedly issued in English, Hindi and Bengali, with no Assamese mentioned. The source is undated and may be stale. — [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/business/1170639-google-partners-cwc-for-flood-forecasting-initiative)

### Inferences
- Sikkim GLOF was mainly a sensing and institutional failure (no EWS, late dam-gate decision). A voice layer would not have prevented it, though rapid voice relay to river-bank villages within the roughly 2-hour window could have helped. Be careful not to overclaim.
- Assam floods are the strongest case for voice. FLEWS gives 12–36 h of lead time at revenue-circle level, which is enough to act on, and the affected populations are huge (lakhs to millions). The open question is the last step: turning a text alert into specific actions that rural Assamese, Bodo, Mising and Bengali-dialect speakers understand, such as where to move, which embankment is at risk, and where the nearest camp is.
- The 4-languages-per-year auto-readout mandate means NE languages such as Bodo, Manipuri, Khasi and Mizo will likely be among the last added. That is a concrete, policy-anchored gap for on-device or low-footprint TTS. This is an inference from the mandate's structure, not a confirmed rollout order.
- Every existing channel (SMS, cell broadcast, Flood Hub) is one-way. No source describes a two-way channel where citizens report a crack, rising water or someone trapped by voice in their own language. This is a hypothesis-level gap.

### Gaps
- No India-specific peer-reviewed study found that measures comprehension of disaster alerts by language.
- No published list of SACHET or cell broadcast languages, and no evidence on whether Bodo, Meitei, Khasi, Mizo or Kokborok alerts have actually been sent.
- No CAG audit of NE early warning located in this pass. A CAG search is worth doing.
- Final 2024 Assam flood totals not confirmed. ASDMA annual data needed.

## Q2. NE India language landscape and AI support status

### Takeaway
The main NE languages have 1–15 million speakers each. Assamese, Bodo and Manipuri are Eighth Schedule languages and get AI4Bharat models (IndicConformer covers all 22 scheduled languages). Khasi, Garo, Mizo, Kokborok and Nagamese are not scheduled and depend on small efforts: IIT Guwahati's Bhashini-funded work and MWire Labs. "Supported" does not mean accurate, and I found no per-language WER for the NE languages.

### Cited Findings
- Census 2011 first-language speakers: Assamese 15,311,351; Bengali 97,237,669 (national); Bodo 1,482,929; Manipuri/Meitei 1,761,079; Nepali 2,926,168. Mother-tongue figures (rounded): Khasi about 1,038,000; Garo about 1,125,000. Mizo had 674,756 speakers and Tripuri/Kokborok 854,023 in 2001; the page lists no 2011 figure for either. — [Wikipedia, compiled from Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_languages_by_number_of_native_speakers_in_India). The primary tables are the [ORGI Census 2011 C-16 catalog](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/10207).
- Secondary claim: Mizo has 830,846 native speakers, which matches the 2011 Lushai/Mizo figure, though the blog gives no year. — [Milestone Localization blog](https://www.milestoneloc.com/languages-of-north-east-india/)
- A January 2026 Bhashini session in Guwahati cited about 200 languages in the region and named Assamese and Mizo as low-resource focus languages. — [IANS](https://ianslive.in/human-capital-meet-in-guwahati-focuses-on-language-ai-bhashini--20260106193354)
- AI4Bharat IndicConformer is ASR for all 22 scheduled languages (MIT licence), with monolingual checkpoints for Bodo (brx), Manipuri (mni) and Santali (sat). — [GitHub](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicConformerASR); [AI4Bharat ASR](https://ai4bharat.iitm.ac.in/areas/asr)
- IIT Guwahati CLST, Bhashini-funded (reported March 2022), is building health-information speech tools for Assamese, Bodo, Manipuri, Khasi, Mizo, Nagamese, Nepali and others. — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/iit-guwahati-researchers-develop-speech-technologies-for-north-eastern-languages-nep-2020/amp)
- MWire Labs "Lemka" offers offline STT and TTS for Nagamese, Khasi, Garo, Mizo, Meitei and Kokborok. Its NE-STT model on AIKosh lists Khasi, Garo, Mizo, Kokborok, Nagamese, Assamese, Chakma and Wancho. — [Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/mwire-labs-launches-voice-ai-system-lemka-for-six-ne-languages); [AIKosh NE-STT](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/models/details/northeast_stt_multilingual_speech_to_text_model.html)

### Inferences
- The gap is quality and domain coverage, not whether a model exists at all. Disaster vocabulary (embankment, revenue circle, relief camp), code-mixing (Assamese-English, Meitei-English) and noisy field audio are unlikely to be covered well by models trained on read speech. This is a hypothesis that needs WER testing.
- The NE tribal languages beyond the big eight (Tangkhul, Thadou-Kuki, Paite, Ao, Lotha, Nyishi, Adi, Karbi, Mising) have almost no speech AI. Many relief-camp populations in Manipur speak Kuki-Chin languages, which is relevant for IIIT Manipur.
- The script for Manipuri (Meitei Mayek or Bengali script) matters for TTS input and alert text.

### Gaps
- No per-language WER or MOS benchmarks found for Bodo, Meitei, Khasi or Mizo in IndicConformer, Bhashini or Lemka.
- No direct statement found on Bhashini's live speech API coverage for Khasi, Garo, Mizo or Kokborok.
- No Census figure for Nagamese, which is a lingua franca and not enumerated separately.

## Q3. NE-specific structural issues: connectivity, shutdowns, displacement, health

### Takeaway
Connectivity and state-imposed shutdowns are a bigger blocker than language in much of the Northeast. About 3,841 villages had no mobile coverage as of mid-2025, and Manipur cut mobile internet for 200+ days in 2023. A voice solution has to work over plain voice calls, IVR, or offline on a device, not over data. Manipur's displaced population of about 50–58k, with documented poor health access, is a real and underserved user group, but trust and the conflict context are serious constraints.

### Cited Findings
- About 3,841 NE villages had no mobile network coverage, and 5,271 had no 4G, per Ministry of Communications data reported in June 2025. Arunachal had the most, with 1,735 villages. The target of 4G in every NE village by December 2023 was missed. — [Sentinel Assam](https://www.sentinelassam.com/amp/story/topheadlines/3841-villages-in-northeast-remain-out-of-mobile-network-coverage); [EastMojo 2022 target](https://eastmojo.com/national/2022/11/08/all-villages-in-ne-to-be-connected-with-4g-network-by-dec-2023-rijiju/); [TRAI NE recommendations, Sept 2023](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-09/Recommendations_23092023.pdf)
- Manipur mobile internet was suspended from 3 May 2023. Broadband was partly restored after 83 days, total shutdown days passed 200 by November 2023, and the shutdown was mostly lifted on 3 Dec 2023 with restrictions kept in conflict zones. A further suspension in five valley districts was reported in April 2026 (snippet only, not verified). — [Maktoob](https://maktoobmedia.com/latest-news/manipur-govt-partially-lifts-internet-ban-after-83-days-mobile-net-remains-snapped/); [Internet Society Pulse](https://pulse.internetsociety.org/blog/internet-shutdown-in-manipur-has-now-crossed-200-days); [internetshutdowns.in](https://internetshutdowns.in/media/Manipur_Extended_120426_60af5fb5-f474-4023-884d-405710f2239d._12b3f67b-f7ed-44ce-ac97-d6_wPV1QyJ.pdf)
- Manipur displacement: more than 70,000 displaced by September 2023. More than 58,000 were in 281 camps per Amnesty (May 2025). About 50,000 were still displaced in December 2025, despite a plan to close about 350 camps by December 2025. — [The Wire/Janata Weekly](https://janataweekly.org/the-manipur-crisis-in-numbers-four-months-of-unending-violence/); [Amnesty, May 2025](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/05/authorities-should-urgently-rehabilitate-thousands-displaced-in-two-years-of-ethnic-violence-in-manipur/); [Newslaundry, Dec 2025](https://www.newslaundry.com/amp/story/2025%2F12%2F08%2Fovercrowded-underfed-manipur-planned-to-shut-relief-camps-in-dec-but-many-still-trapped); [Organiser, Jul 2025](https://organiser.org/2025/07/05/301168/bharat/manipur-first-signs-of-normalcy-as-govt-plans-to-shut-relief-camps-in-phased-manner-after-2023-violence/amp/)
- An XCEPT briefing (October 2025) covering 13 camp settings found that violence cut off access to key health hubs, especially emergency and maternal care, which pushed displaced people toward traditional healers and birth attendants. — [XCEPT](https://www.xcept-research.org/publication/unseen-and-unrecorded-the-impact-of-conflict-and-forced-displacement-on-access-to-healthcare-for-women-and-children-in-manipur/)
- Advocacy and media reports describe camp residents with cancer, TB or kidney failure unable to reach specialist care, and outbreaks of measles and dysentery in camps. These are advocacy sources, so treat accordingly. — [Newsreel Asia](https://newsreel.asia/articles/manipur-violence-displaced-cancer-kidney-failure); [Deccan Herald (doctors' team)](https://deccanherald.com/india/manipur/team-of-doctors-visits-manipur-raises-concerns-over-health-situation-in-relief-camps-2672613)

### Inferences
- In Manipur, Arunachal and the hill districts, the binding constraint is often connectivity or policy, not language. Any voice solution must degrade gracefully: offline on-device, voice-call or IVR when data is off, and SMS/cell broadcast fallback. Mobile data shutdowns do not normally cut voice calls or SMS, so IVR is a practical channel. This is an inference; check whether voice was also restricted in any district.
- Relief camps are a concentrated, multilingual (Meitei and Kuki-Chin languages), low-literacy-mix population with acute information needs: health camp schedules, entitlement claims, missing-person and grievance registration. Ethnic polarisation makes language choice and data handling sensitive. A Meitei-only system would be distrusted in Kuki-Zo camps, and the reverse holds too. Trust is the blocker as much as technology.
- I did not research agriculture (tea, horticulture), tourism or general government-service access in this pass for lack of time budget. They are listed as gaps.

### Gaps
- Tea-garden workers (Assam), horticulture advisory and tourism use cases: no sourced findings gathered.
- No data found on literacy or smartphone ownership specifically among NE flood-affected or camp populations.
- No source found confirming whether voice calls were restricted in Manipur shutdowns.

## Q4. Existing projects and startups; where voice AI is genuinely needed (skeptical assessment)

### Takeaway
Supply-side efforts exist: AI4Bharat, IIT Guwahati CLST, MWire Labs Lemka, Bhashini, Google Flood Hub and SOS alerts, SACHET and cell broadcast. None of them, as found, combines official alerts with two-way local-language voice for NE disaster response. The defensible niche is narrow and specific: a voice/IVR layer over existing official feeds (FLEWS, SACHET CAP), in Assamese, Bodo, Meitei and selected tribal languages, that works when data is down.

### Cited Findings
- During the 2022 Assam floods, Google added SOS alerts in Maps and Search showing relief-camp locations and helplines, and donated $500k. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/technology/assam-floods-google-brings-sos-crisis-alerts-help-details-on-maps-search-apps-1122092.html); [Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/assam-floods-google-launches-crisis-response-efforts-details-here-339464-2022-06-28)
- Google partners with CWC on flood forecasting and sends alerts to Android users with location enabled. — [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/business/1170639-google-partners-cwc-for-flood-forecasting-initiative)
- MWire Labs Lemka (offline, 6 NE languages), IIT Guwahati CLST (health speech tools, 10 languages), and AI4Bharat IndicConformer (22 scheduled languages) are covered above. — [Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/mwire-labs-launches-voice-ai-system-lemka-for-six-ne-languages); [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/iit-guwahati-researchers-develop-speech-technologies-for-north-eastern-languages-nep-2020/amp); [GitHub](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicConformerASR)

### Inferences (candidate problems, ranked by how strongly voice AI is needed)
1. **Assam flood voice alert and two-way IVR: strong.** Who suffers: rural riverine households in Lakhimpur, Dhemaji, Barpeta, Nagaon and Cachar (Barak valley). Scale: 11–55 lakh affected in peak years (2022, 2024). Current tools: FLEWS → ASDMA → SACHET SMS, Google alerts. Gap: text only, one-way, uncertain coverage of Assamese dialects and Bodo, Mising and Bengali. Need: genuine, because 12–36 h of lead time is enough to act and language/literacy limit comprehension. Risk: ASDMA must agree to integrate, since official authority is essential for trust.
2. **TTS readout of cell broadcast/CAP in NE languages for feature phones: strong and policy-anchored.** The DoT mandate already requires readout and adds languages slowly, and memory limits on feature phones are a known industry blocker. A small on-device TTS for Bodo, Meitei, Khasi and Mizo addresses that directly. Risk: requires telecom/OEM partnerships.
3. **Landslide hyperlocal warning and reporting in hill states (Mizoram, Manipur, Sikkim): moderate.** The upstream problem is sensing and susceptibility mapping, which is where the participant's prior disaster-risk monitoring work fits. Voice helps on community reporting (cracks, seepage) and relaying slope-specific alerts in Mizo or Meitei. Risk: no reliable slope-level forecast exists to relay. Language is not the first blocker.
4. **Manipur relief-camp voice helpdesk (health, entitlements, grievances): moderate need, high trust and political risk.** It must work over IVR during data shutdowns and must be multilingual across ethnic lines.
5. **GLOF-type flash floods: weak for voice.** The failure was sensing and institutional decision-making, and voice is at most a relay add-on.

### Gaps
- No evaluation found of any deployed local-language voice/IVR disaster alert system in the Northeast. Field validation with ASDMA or a district DDMA is needed.
- No startup found specifically doing disaster voice AI for the Northeast. Absence of evidence after a limited search, not proof.
